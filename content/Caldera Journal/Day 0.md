I have spent over two weeks on this boat wondering when I should place things in this journal. Man Willham and Woman Lae were quite cryptic on when I should start recording the ways of Calderans. When I asked them if I should read the seven previous missionary outings, they gave me brusque no’s without further explanation. Grandfather Pierce, if you read this upon my arrival I would like you to know that your insight of “All missionaries must go about their duties with untainted opinions is how we achieve unwarped facts.” gave me the much needed relief of being able to sleep.

Since I have no guidance on what to enter, I figured I would dedicate this first entry to describing my first real interactions with Calderans on this boat. 

For one,  Captain Ezrich is lionized by his crew. He is quite agreeable amongst the passengers as well and has set up personal dinners with me weekly. Since we are making good time, he has slowed the boat down to make our approach into Bronson in the night. When I asked him why and told him that I needed to find room and board his tin like laugh was so loud that I could swear the lights in the room flickered subtly brighter with every guffaw. 

He reassured me that my city guide would still be at the docks waiting to take me to my residence.  
“Boy,” Captain Ezrich told me. “Bronson never closes and I want to see your face when you see it.”

Mind you, that is what I perceived from his accent. For the sake of my sanity and for the reason of this journal to be a catalog of experience and knowledge, I will not try to write out the exact words of Calderans. If you are curious, though it sounded more like this.  
“Bae, Bwongsin naer sleeps an eh want to see yuh faze wing yuh see et.”

It pained me to write that. 

The fact that Caldera was founded by fellow countrymen of Lenwing over 400 years ago, I am shocked at how much those years took such liberties on Len’s language. Maybe it’s because they stopped believing in him and he cursed their speech.
