
## Summary
Chances are if you want to be a writer its because you like to read. There is probably some genres you want to write in that you read avidly. If this is the case then we want to understand why we love the books we do.

## Text Books:
- Reading Like a Writer: A Guide for People Who Love Books and for Those Who Want to Write Them by Francine Prose
- Your Favorite Book

## Peer interaction:
- [Scribophile ](https://www.scribophile.com/)

## Homework Phase 1
### Daily Homework:
- Write at least 20 minutes a day
- Read One chapter of *Reading Like a Writer* a day
	- Take notes, ingest the information don't just glance it.
- Come up with one story idea a day and log it in a journal. Even if you are not feeling the muse, even if it seems campy and embarrassing, no one needs to read it but you.

### Weekly Homework:
- Read and review one piece of work on Scribophile.
- Write one short story or scene (What you are doing with the 20 minutes of writing)
	- Enter writing contests if you need a deadline.

### Homework Phase 2
Daily Homework:
- Write at least 20 minutes a day
- Come up with one story idea a day and log it in a journal. Even if you are not feeling the muse, even if it seems campy and embarrassing, no one needs to read it but you.

Weekly Homework:
- Read and review one piece of work on Scribophile.
- Write one short story or scene (What you are doing with the 20 minutes of writing)
	- Enter writing contests if you need a deadline.
- Read one chapter from your favorite book taking your time and using what you learned from *Reading Like a Writer*.
- Rewrite your favorite paragraph from the book you are reading from memory. Compare it to the original. Compare the differences.
  
## When do I move on?
When you finish the books
