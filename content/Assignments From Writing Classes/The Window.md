One thing you must know about working in sales that I have learned over the course of my career is that the Holliday party dictates how well we did. If they throw the party in the office and the caterer is not somewhere, you’d take a client, then there will be layoffs. Hosting it on location with a travel budget probably means layoffs are coming, but they don’t want to worry their highest achievers. The safest place is one of the top hotels in the city.

This party was at the Ritz-Carlton, which meant only the lowest achievers were getting the guillotine. The smart ones were already looking for a new job to start the first quarter, while the less experienced ones mingled with the high achievers, hoping that a computer algorithm would not catch them through osmosis. 

Frank could have retired already with his bonuses in the millions. The company demoted him from president to account executive after he failed to help up sale an account that made up ten percent of the company’s profits. 

He came into the dining room smelling of alcohol, a bright pink golf polo and turquoise golf pants making him look like a neon sign in a sea of penguins. He ignored the half-waves given to him while he trudged his way to the bar. This would be his last social gathering here, and I fully expected it to go as well as he looked. He was already vocal about the shakeup in leadership, hiring a young Harvard graduate that interned and McKinsey. They learned by now not to have a mic at these events.

Frank was my mentor. I followed him from company to company and was usually his first hire when he made his own shakeups. I owed it to him to see him leave and try to convince him to go out with some level of dignity.

“I think I am going to start a goose farm,” Frank grumbled when he saw me approach. “We have our farmhouse I have been meaning to renovate.”

“Is there a market for geese?” I said with a chuckle while making gestures at the bartender I would have what Frank was having.

“Is there a market?” Frank scoffed, “I have my connections at Columbia that would buy the down and then there is the meat itself.” 

Frank looked at me with narrowed eyes. I was not his direct report anymore, so I didn’t have to worry about pushing on his pride.  
“And when was the last time you ate a goose? I don’t even know where to buy one.”  
“Go to any store and ask at the meat counter. They have them frozen in the back.” Frank said, swirling his negroni. The flame in his tone was snuffed out by my logic.

“I think you would be a great goose farmer,” I said while patting him on the back and giving him a reassuring smile. “I would love to come help.”
