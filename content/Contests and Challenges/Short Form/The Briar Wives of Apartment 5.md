The two women, the room, and the light felt frozen. The air had the scent of citrus cleaners while carrying with it an uneasy weight of waiting to spread any vibration of sound the two women sitting next to each other would make. Through single-pane bay windows, the half-asleep sun shed its last light, catching specks of dust as they swirled around looking for a place to rest. The orange-tinged light rubbed onto porcelain and crystal oddities that matched the gilded cream and gold paisley loveseat.

It was rare to see Ashley Briar sit still. She was the type of woman who started a conversation mid-thought and preferred to say her goodbyes with the sound of her boots walking away. Yet she sat there looking out the window, realising her final blood relative had passed. She was the last Kinsey, a surname she had shed like uncomfortable clothes.

Anya never let Ashley know that she sometimes heard her late at night leaving voicemails to her mom. She heard the child awaken in Ashley’s voice as she horribly described the inside jokes they formed and the mid-century Pyrex dishes they found in flea markets. With every peek in her voice, the crashing of silence came as Anya knew Ashley waited, hoping her mother would call her back before she finished. Every time Anya heard, “I love you, Mom, goodbye” filling a long pause, she would get angry.

She prepped for this moment. Twenty years ago, they had visited Ashley’s mother for the last time to inform her they were not just college roommates. Ashley got her intensity from her mother, and it was on full display that day as the sound of screaming drowned out any noise Anya could ever make. She had snuck into the kitchen and had taken a picture of the recipe for Curried Chicken Casserole. Ashley had always talked about this dish with fond memories as she bragged about how great a cook her mother was. Anya didn’t have the heart to tell her it was a recipe from the back of a can of Campbell’s Cream of Chicken soup from 1974.

“Would you like to go to the store with me?”

“Absolutely,” Ashley replied with a faded smirk, slapping her thighs as she stood. Ashley was halfway to the door before Anya even had her cardigan on. On any other day this would be normal behavior, but today it felt forced.
